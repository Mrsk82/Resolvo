Jira Service Management's Free Tier Caps at 3 Agents — Then What?

For a lot of 2-3 person support teams, Jira Service Management's free plan is the first real helpdesk they use — and it's a genuinely capable one. But the ceiling is closer than it looks. Here's exactly what's included, what breaks first, and what a next step looks like if you're honest about the tradeoffs.

By Resolvo Team · 6 min read · September 2026

What Jira Service Management's free tier actually includes

Atlassian's free plan for Jira Service Management is usage-capped, not feature-stripped — you get a real product, but with hard ceilings on the things that matter most as a team grows:

  • 3 agents — the seats that can actually work tickets, not just view them.
  • 2GB storage — attachments, screenshots, and logs count against this.
  • 100 emails/day — inbound email-to-ticket volume, across your whole team.
  • 500 automation runs/month — every auto-assignment rule, notification, or SLA escalation counts as a run.
  • No vendor support — free-tier customers rely on Atlassian's community forums and public documentation, not a support ticket to Atlassian itself.

For a 2-agent team fielding a modest volume of tickets, this is often plenty. The problem is that none of these limits move gradually — you tend to hit them right around the point where your team is actually growing, which is the worst possible time for a support tool to start pushing back.

⚠ The sneakiest limit The 500 automation runs/month cap is easy to miss because it doesn't fail loudly. Automation rules just stop firing once you're over — tickets stop auto-assigning, SLA reminders stop sending — and unless someone's actively watching for it, that can go unnoticed for a while.

The moment you outgrow it

A few concrete signs that the free tier is no longer the right fit:

  • You're hiring a 4th agent, even part-time or temporary.
  • Your daily email volume is creeping toward 100/day — a single busy Monday can burn through it.
  • Automation rules are silently not firing because you've crossed 500 runs for the month.
  • Something breaks and you need an actual answer from Atlassian, not a community thread.

What Jira Service Management costs once you're past free

Once you outgrow the free tier, JSM's published pricing moves to Standard at roughly $20-24/agent/month, and Premium at roughly $47-55/agent/month. For a 5-agent team, that's a jump from $0/month to somewhere around $100-120/month at minimum on Standard — before any marketplace add-ons. These are the USD figures published by Atlassian and tracked by third-party pricing trackers; no India-specific (₹) pricing has been found on Atlassian's own pages, so budget in USD terms unless you confirm an India rate directly with Atlassian.

It's not just the price — the complexity comes with it

Independent reviews of Jira Service Management consistently describe it as admin-dependent to configure properly, with a real learning curve and setup that can stretch from weeks to months for anything beyond the basics — a pattern that shows up in both professional reviews and discussion threads on Atlassian's own community forums. That complexity doesn't go away when you start paying; if anything, the paid tiers unlock more configuration surface area (workflow schemes, permission schemes, SLA rules) that a small team without a dedicated admin will spend real time learning.

What to actually evaluate before you pick a next tool

Whether you stay with Jira Service Management's paid tiers or look elsewhere, these are the questions worth asking honestly:

  • Is it a permanent free tier or a time-limited trial? Don't treat the two as equivalent when planning your budget.
  • Does the entry paid tier assume a dedicated admin? If setup and maintenance require someone learning workflow/permission schemes, that's a real ongoing cost even if the subscription price looks fine.
  • Is the channel your customers actually use — WhatsApp, email, chat — native, or a separate purchase/integration? Jira Service Management has no native WhatsApp channel; teams that need it are relying on third-party connectors rather than something Atlassian ships directly.
  • Does pricing scale predictably per agent, or does it stack with add-ons as you grow?
