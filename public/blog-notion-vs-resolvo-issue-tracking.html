Notion vs Resolvo for Issue Tracking: Which Is Better for Your Team?

Notion is one of the most popular tools in Indian startups — flexible, familiar, and free to start. But using Notion as an issue tracker is a bit like using a Swiss Army knife as a kitchen knife. It works, until you really need to cook. Here's the honest comparison.

By Resolvo Team · 7 min read · June 2025

How teams use Notion for issue tracking

The typical Notion issue tracker is a database with columns for Status, Assignee, Priority, and Description. It works surprisingly well for small teams in the early stages — everyone is familiar with Notion, setup takes an hour, and there's no new tool to learn.

The cracks appear as the team and ticket volume grows: filtering gets unwieldy, sprint grouping requires manual workarounds, there are no SLA alerts, no automatic assignment rules, and no integration with your support inbox. You end up spending as much time maintaining the Notion database as using it.

Where Notion is genuinely better

  • Flexibility — Notion can be shaped into almost anything. If your issue tracking needs are unusual, you can build exactly what you need
  • Documentation alongside issues — teams that want their issue tracker and their engineering wiki in the same tool benefit from Notion's document capabilities
  • Familiarity — if your whole team already lives in Notion, adding an issue database creates no context switching
  • Free tier — Notion's free plan is generous for small teams

Where Resolvo is better for issue tracking

  • Purpose-built workflows — open → in progress → review → done is automatic, not a manual column change
  • SLA by priority — Critical bugs get alerts when they've been open too long. Notion has no concept of SLA
  • Customer support connection — when a customer emails about a bug, it becomes a tracked issue automatically. In Notion, someone has to manually copy the report in
  • Sprint management — native sprint grouping, velocity tracking, and sprint close reports without database gymnastics
  • Reporting — how many bugs were opened this week? What's the average time to close a Critical issue? These take seconds in Resolvo and require manual spreadsheet work in Notion

⚠️ The Notion maintenance problem: As your issue volume grows, someone becomes the unofficial "Notion admin" who spends 30–60 minutes/week keeping the database clean, archiving old issues, and fixing broken filters. This hidden cost doesn't show up in the feature comparison but is very real.

When to stick with Notion

Notion issue tracking is the right choice if:

  • You're a team of 1–3 people with fewer than 20 open issues at any time
  • You don't need SLA tracking or sprint management
  • You want your docs and issues in the same place
  • Your team already has strong Notion habits and switching would cause friction

When to switch to Resolvo

Switch when:

  • Your Notion issue database has more than 100 rows and is getting hard to navigate
  • Issues are slipping through because nobody has visibility into what's overdue
  • You're running sprints and need proper sprint management
  • Customer-reported bugs come via email and need to be linked to engineering issues
  • Your team lead is spending significant time maintaining the Notion database
