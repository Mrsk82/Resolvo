Free CRM for Small Business India: What to Use in 2025

Most CRM software is built for 50-person sales teams with complex pipelines and deal stages. A small Indian business — a consultant, a retailer, a service firm — needs something much simpler. Here's what that looks like.

By Resolvo Team · 8 min read · June 2025

What a CRM is (and what it isn't)

CRM stands for Customer Relationship Management. In practice, it's a system that answers three questions:

  • Who are my customers and leads?
  • What's the history of my relationship with each of them?
  • What do I need to do next for each one?

A CRM is not a billing system, not a marketing automation platform, and not a social media tool — though many enterprise CRMs try to be all of these things. For a small business, those extras are noise.

Do you actually need a CRM?

You need a CRM if you answered yes to any of these:

  • You've ever forgotten to follow up with a hot lead
  • A client asked you something and you had no record of your previous conversation
  • You can't tell which clients are most valuable to your business
  • You don't know which clients are at risk of churning or going quiet
  • You have more than 20 active client relationships

If you have fewer than 10 clients and interact with all of them regularly, a spreadsheet may genuinely be enough. The right tool is the simplest one that works.

What a small business CRM needs to do

For most small Indian businesses, a CRM needs exactly these capabilities:

1. Contact management

Store name, phone, email, company, and any custom fields relevant to your business (service category, city, acquisition source). Search and filter contacts quickly. That's it.

2. Interaction history

Every time you email, call, or meet a client, log a note. When you open the contact next month, you see exactly what was discussed. No more "I think we spoke in March but I don't remember what about."

3. Follow-up reminders

The single most valuable CRM feature for small businesses. Log: "Follow up about proposal on 5 July." The CRM reminds you. Nothing falls through the cracks.

4. Deal or pipeline tracking (optional)

If you have a sales process — leads → qualified → proposal sent → won/lost — a pipeline view is useful. If you don't have a defined sales process, skip this for now.

💡 Start with 3 things: contact records, interaction notes, and follow-up reminders. Everything else is optional until you feel the pain that requires it.

The problem with most "free" CRMs

The free tier of most enterprise CRMs (Salesforce, HubSpot, Zoho) gives you just enough to get hooked, then locks the features you actually need behind a paid tier. The most common pattern:

  • Free: contact storage + basic notes
  • Paid (₹1,500–₹3,000/user/month): email integration, follow-up reminders, reporting, and everything that makes a CRM useful

For a 2-person consultancy or a solo service professional, paying ₹3,000/month per user for Salesforce is absurd. You need a solution that's genuinely free or very inexpensive at small scale.

How to migrate from a spreadsheet to a CRM

Most small businesses start with a spreadsheet. Moving to a CRM is easier than it sounds:

  1. Export your contacts from the spreadsheet as CSV
  2. Import into the CRM — most support CSV import with field mapping
  3. For your top 20 active clients, add any important history notes manually
  4. Start using the follow-up reminder feature immediately for all active leads

The import takes an hour. The habit of logging interactions takes 2 weeks to form. After that, it's automatic.

What to do in the first week

  1. Import all contacts
  2. Set a follow-up reminder for every active lead or client
  3. Log one note for each of your top 10 clients (what they're using you for, any open questions)
  4. At the end of each day, spend 5 minutes logging that day's client interactions

After 30 days, you'll have a system that tells you exactly who needs attention today and a complete history of every client relationship you have.
