Online Appointment Booking for Dentists in India: Setup Guide

The average dental clinic in India loses 8–12 appointment slots per week to missed calls, no-shows, and double bookings. Online booking solves all three. Here's how to set it up for your practice in under an hour — free.

By Resolvo Team · 7 min read · June 2025

Why dental clinics still rely on phone calls (and why it hurts)

Most dental clinics still take appointments over the phone. This creates three problems:

  • Missed bookings — patients call during treatment hours, nobody answers, they call a competitor instead
  • No-shows — a dental appointment booked 3 days ago over the phone is often forgotten by the patient
  • Receptionist overload — 30–40% of a receptionist's time goes to appointment management, leaving less time for patient experience

Online booking with automated reminders directly addresses all three. Patients book at any time, receive reminders 24 hours and 2 hours before, and your receptionist is free to focus on patients who are actually in the clinic.

What dental patients specifically need from online booking

Dental appointments have unique requirements compared to general clinics:

  • Appointment type selection — checkup, cleaning, filling, root canal, emergency — each has a different duration that needs to block the right amount of time
  • New vs returning patient — new patients need longer slots (45–60 min) vs returning (15–30 min)
  • Treatment chair availability — if you have 2 chairs and 2 dentists, you may need to manage availability across chairs
  • Emergency slots — keep 1–2 slots per day open for emergency/walk-in cases

Setting up appointment types in Resolvo

Create separate appointment types for each procedure category:

  1. Checkup & Consultation — 30 minutes, allow online booking
  2. Cleaning (Scaling) — 45 minutes, allow online booking
  3. Filling — 45–60 minutes, allow online booking
  4. Root Canal Consultation — 30 minutes, allow online booking (full procedure is by consultation only)
  5. Emergency / Toothache — 30 minutes, 2 reserved slots per day
  6. New Patient Consultation — 60 minutes, allow online booking

💡 Don't list procedures with variable duration as bookable online. Anything that might run 30 minutes or 3 hours (like complex extractions or full crown work) should be consultation-first. Let patients book the consultation online; you schedule the procedure after assessment.

Reducing dental no-shows with automated reminders

Dental appointments have higher no-show rates than most medical appointments because they're discretionary — patients postpone when the toothache isn't acute. The reminder sequence that works:

  • Confirmation: Immediately after booking — "Your appointment at [Clinic] is confirmed for [Date] at [Time]"
  • Reminder 1: 48 hours before — "Your dental appointment is in 2 days. Reply CANCEL if you cannot make it."
  • Reminder 2: 2 hours before — "Your appointment is today at [Time]. [Clinic address + Google Maps link]"

Clinics using this 3-touchpoint sequence typically see no-show rates drop from 25–30% to under 10%.

Your booking link: where to put it

A booking link that nobody can find doesn't help. Put it everywhere:

  • WhatsApp Business auto-reply ("Hi! Book your appointment here: [link]")
  • Google Business Profile — add as "Book Online" button
  • Your Instagram/Facebook bio
  • Your clinic's website contact page
  • The out-of-hours message on your phone

Handling cancellations and late reschedules

Set a cancellation policy in your booking confirmation. For a dental practice, 24-hour cancellation notice is reasonable. Make the policy visible when patients book and again in the reminder:

"If you need to cancel or reschedule, please do so at least 24 hours before your appointment. [Cancel/reschedule link]"

Don't charge for first-time cancellations — relationships matter more than one missed fee. But a patient who cancels twice within 30 days can be flagged as high no-show risk and may receive a reminder call from your receptionist rather than just automated messages.
