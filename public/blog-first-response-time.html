First Response Time: Why It's Your Most Important Support Metric

Customers can wait for a full resolution if they know someone is on it. What they can't tolerate is silence. First response time — how long until you acknowledge a ticket — drives customer satisfaction more than resolution time, and it's the easiest metric to improve quickly.

By Resolvo Team · 6 min read · June 2025

What first response time (FRT) actually measures

FRT is the time between when a customer submits a support ticket and when they receive their first reply from a human agent. It does not include automated acknowledgement emails — those don't count because customers know they're automated and discount them.

A good first response doesn't have to fully solve the problem. "Hi Priya, I've received your message about the order delay. Let me check this and get back to you within 2 hours with an update" is a perfectly valid first response. It acknowledges the customer, shows a human read their message, and sets a clear expectation for the next step.

Why FRT matters more than resolution time

Research consistently shows that customers rate their support experience more on acknowledgement speed than on resolution speed. A customer who receives a warm, empathetic acknowledgement in 30 minutes — even if the full resolution takes 4 hours — rates the experience much higher than one who receives a complete resolution in 2 hours but felt ignored in the first hour.

The psychological mechanism is simple: silence creates anxiety. Once you acknowledge, the customer's anxiety drops significantly. They know someone is working on it. They can go back to their day.

FRT benchmarks by channel and priority

  • Critical issues (payments down, data loss): under 30 minutes
  • High priority (feature broken, significant impact): under 2 hours
  • Medium priority (partial issue, workaround available): under 8 hours
  • Low priority (feature request, general question): under 24 hours

For Indian support teams, aim to acknowledge all tickets before the end of the business day they arrive. A ticket that arrives at 5:30pm and is acknowledged at 9:00am the next day is a 15.5-hour FRT — that's acceptable for a low-priority ticket but unacceptable for anything urgent.

💡 Weekend FRT: Set clear expectations on your support page about weekend hours. "We respond Monday–Saturday, 9am–7pm IST" is better than leaving customers uncertain about whether anyone will respond to a Friday evening email.

5 ways to improve your FRT this week

1. Set up auto-acknowledgement with a real commitment

An auto-reply that says "We received your message and will respond within 24 hours" is far better than silence. It resets the customer's anxiety clock immediately and sets a clear expectation. Make sure your team actually meets the commitment stated in the auto-reply.

2. Use a shared queue, not individual inboxes

If customer emails go to individual agents, tickets sit unread when that agent is busy or offline. A shared queue with assigned tickets means someone is always responsible for monitoring incoming tickets and grabbing unassigned ones.

3. Build a first-response template library

The most common ticket types (order issues, billing questions, technical problems) should have pre-written first-response templates. An agent can acknowledge in 60 seconds instead of writing from scratch. Personalise, but don't start from zero.

4. Set SLA alerts at 50% of your target time

If your FRT target is 4 hours, set an alert at 2 hours. This gives agents time to respond before the SLA is breached, rather than finding out after it's already missed.

5. Check the queue at the start of every shift

Make reviewing the queue the first task of every shift — before checking Slack, before any other work. Tickets that arrived at the end of the previous shift need a response first thing.

When FRT is misleading

FRT can be gamed: agents send a one-line "Got it, looking into this" and then take 6 hours to actually engage with the problem. Watch reopen rate and CSAT alongside FRT to catch this pattern. Fast FRT + low CSAT = agents are acknowledging quickly but not resolving properly.

Communicating FRT to customers

State your FRT commitment on your website contact page, in your auto-reply, and in your email signature. "We respond to all queries within 4 hours on business days" sets an expectation that customers can hold you to — and that motivates the team to meet it.
