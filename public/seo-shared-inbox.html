A shared Gmail inbox is a good start

Most small teams begin the same way. There is a support@ address, three people have the password, and everyone replies when they see something. It costs nothing, and for the first few months it works fine.

Then it quietly stops working. Nothing breaks. You just start finding out about problems from customers instead of from your own inbox.

Signs you have outgrown it

  • Two people answer the same customer. Or nobody does, because each assumed the other had it.
  • You cannot tell what is waiting. An unread count is not a to-do list. Read does not mean handled.
  • Nobody knows what is about to go late. There is no reply deadline, so the angry email from Tuesday is found on Friday.
  • Context lives in one person's head. When they are on leave, the history of that customer goes with them.
  • Newsletters and alerts bury the real emails. Your support address ends up on mailing lists, and the signal is lost in the noise.
  • WhatsApp is a separate world. Half your customers message a phone that one person carries.

If three or more of these sound familiar, the inbox is costing you customers, even if you cannot see the number yet.

What a support inbox needs that Gmail does not have

  • An owner on every conversation, so you can see who has it.
  • A status (new, waiting on us, waiting on the customer, done) that is not just read or unread.
  • A reply deadline with a warning before it passes.
  • Internal notes your team can write on a conversation without the customer seeing them.
  • One customer record with their earlier conversations, whichever channel they used.
  • Canned replies for the answers you type ten times a week.

How to move in an afternoon

  1. Connect your support email. Keep the same address. Customers notice nothing.
  2. Invite your team. Adding people does not change the price.
  3. Set a reply target. For example, first reply within 4 working hours.
  4. Add three canned replies for the questions you answer most.
  5. Answer one real email from the new inbox, and you are live.

The email inbox is part of the Pro plan (₹999 a month per brand), and you can try it free for 14 days. Resolvo also keeps newsletters and automated alerts out of your queue and your counts, and you can close them in one click.

When Gmail is still fine

If one person handles under ten emails a day and there is no WhatsApp, a shared inbox is enough. Do not buy a helpdesk you do not need. Come back when a second person joins, or when something goes missing for the first time.
